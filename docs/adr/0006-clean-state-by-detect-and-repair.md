# Clean state by detect-and-repair

Users leave things on Switches that a Cleanup didn't undo. On 10.69.144.132 (2026-10-12) there were local users, shell and Python histories, scripts in `/flash/python`, crash dumps, a `running.cfg`, and a `vcsetup.cfg` copied into certified. A read of every lab Switch the same day found the same everywhere: AOS images and tools in user folders, captures, packet dumps, and an init changed by hand. The rule is now that once a Reservation ends, nothing of it stays: only AOS's own files and what the lab managers need.

AOS can't stop a user from writing there. It has no permission per folder, and a user who may configure the Switch may also write to `/flash` and `init/`. Weaker Switch accounts would block the work people reserve Switches for. So BLab **detects and repairs**: the Inspection compares the Switch with its clean state, and the Cleanup brings it back to it.

## The clean state

- **Baseline**: what a clean Switch holds under `/flash`, as patterns in `api/clean_state.py`. It was built from 132 right after its Cleanup and from a read-only listing of every reachable lab Switch (OS6900, OS6860E, OS6860N, OS6870, OS9900; AOS 8.9 to 8.11). There is one list for every family: what one family's AOS writes is simply absent on the others. Logs AOS writes itself (`swlog*`, `*.log`, `tech_support*`, `*.err`...) and license files are in it. Anything we weren't sure AOS writes is in it too: a file left behind costs less than a Switch that won't boot.
- **Only some directories are compared**: `/flash` itself, `.ssh`, `python`, `pmd` and `init`, and only their own entries. AOS's own trees (`switch/`, `system/`, `network/`...) are never looked into. `working/` and `certified/` aren't either, because every Cleanup replaces them from `init/`.
- **Extras**: the lab managers' files and logins are `BaselineExtra` rows that an admin edits on the admin site. A Cleanup leaves them in place, and an Inspection doesn't count them.
- **Local users**: every local user but `admin`, AOS's `default` template and the extra logins is Unwanted. That covers Switch accounts BLab failed to remove, and any account made by hand.
- **`init/` extras**: anything in `init/` besides its AOS images, `pkg/`, `vcboot.cfg` and the Golden VC setup is Unwanted, such as a `vcsetup.cfg`, `boot.md5` or `cloudagent.cfg` copied there (found on 10.69.144.168 and .169): AOS writes those in `working/` at boot, and a `vcsetup.cfg` copied into init is how one from another Switch reached certified on 132.
- **Golden init**: `init/vcboot.cfg` must be the lab template that `prepare_switches` writes, named after the Switch's model. Trailing spaces, blank lines and `!` comments don't count. `init/` must also hold an AOS image (`*.img`) and `pkg/`. Which release that image is, and how a Cleanup installs it: ADR 0011. A Switch recorded without a model has no Golden init: its init config is neither compared nor rewritten.
- **Golden VC setup** (#94): `init/vcsetup.cfg` must hold exactly these two lines:
  `virtual-chassis chassis-id 1 configured-chassis-id 1` and `virtual-chassis vf-link-mode static` (`auto` until #107). A Cleanup empties `working/`, and a Switch that boots without a `vcsetup.cfg` gets AOS's default VC setup: its last ports become auto-VFL ports, which are kept for VC links and never carry a Link. Every lab Switch read on 2026-11-03 had them: 1/1/25 to 1/1/28A on the OS6900-T24C2s 10.69.144.140 and .141, 1/1/50A to 54A on 132, and the last two ports on the OS6860E and the OS6870s. On 132 (OS6900-X48C6, AOS 8.11.162.R01), a boot with only those two lines in `working/vcsetup.cfg` left `show virtual-chassis auto-vf-link-port` empty. So the Inspection reads that command, and an auto-VFL port on a Switch nobody holds is a finding. In auto mode, an OS6860N-P24Z still made 1/1/25-26 auto-VFL with no `auto-vf-link-port` line in its file (#107); in static mode AOS picks none: on 132 (2026-11-04), booting the two static lines from a `working/` whose auto-VFL ports were 1/1/50A-54A left none, and AOS kept the file as written. The file is judged on what it does, not its wording (#109): AOS's standalone defaults (`chassis-id 1 configured-chassis-id 1`, `vf-link-mode auto` or `static`, the `chassis-id 1 chassis-group <n>` it adds), comments, and their order or absence are fine, so a missing file is clean as long as no port is auto-VFL; any other VC line (`auto-vf-link-port`, another chassis-id, a priority, a VFL), and a missing or extra management line, is a finding. A line-by-line check made 33 good Switches Not clean on 2026-11-04. A Cleanup still rewrites the file whenever it isn't the Golden VC setup word for word, so the lab converges on it without a Cleanup of its own. Except on a VC: when `show chassis` lists more than one chassis (or can't be read), the Cleanup leaves `init/vcsetup.cfg` as it is, since static mode with no VFL would split the VC at the reload; its Inspection already says it is in a VC, for an admin to settle. Members may still set up a VC during their Reservation; the Cleanup puts it back. A chassis (OS99xx, whose CMM port is listed: .171), the OS2260 Management switches (no `virtual-chassis` command: .133) and a Switch without a model have no Golden VC setup, so their `vcsetup.cfg` in `init/` stays Unwanted, as before. `api.clean_state.golden_vc_setup` is the one place that decides it.
- **Management in the Golden VC setup** (#81): a Switch without an EMP has a front port as its Management port (`Switch.management_port`, empty for an EMP). Its Golden VC setup adds `vlan 1443 admin-state enable`, `vlan 1443 name "BLab management"`, `vlan 1443 members port <port> untagged` and `ip interface "management" address <its address> mask <its subnet's> vlan 1443`, so every Cleanup brings its management back, and an Inspection compares it like the rest. On 132 (AOS 8.11.162.R01, 2026-11-04), AOS took VLAN, `ip interface` and `ip static-route` lines from `working/vcsetup.cfg` at boot. A `write memory` moves them into `working/vcboot.cfg` and leaves `init/` alone, so the next Cleanup still copies `init/vcsetup.cfg` back. The routes stay in the Golden init. A member who deletes VLAN 1443 or the interface and saves cuts BLab off the Switch until its console is reached, the same exposure as any management setup without an EMP. The Management port counts as cabled on purpose, so its link being up is never an Unwanted cable.
- **The Golden init's routes** go through the gateway of the Switch's own subnet of the Management network (`BLAB_MANAGEMENT_NETWORKS`); a Switch in none keeps 10.69.144.129 (#81).
- **SU**: a local user other than `admin` and the extras with `SU allowed` (AOS 8.10) can open a root shell. The Inspection reports it, and as a warning even on a reserved Switch. Switch accounts are created without it (checked on 132, AOS 8.10.86.R04).

## How it is repaired

The Switch worker reads the Switch before each Cleanup and works out what to repair. The Cleanup then does the following, in this order. It removes the Unwanted local users with `no user <name>`, which refuses `admin` and `default`. If init's config isn't the Golden init, it rewrites it, and it doesn't reload if that fails. It rewrites `init/vcsetup.cfg` the same way when it isn't the Golden VC setup. The usual copy of `init/` into `working/` then makes the Switch boot with it, which also clears the auto-VFL ports members set up. It removes each Unwanted path with `rm -rf -- '/flash/<path>'`, but only for entries of the compared directories that are outside the Baseline. It checks that `init/` holds what a boot needs before it empties `working/`. Then it restores init and reloads, as before.

A copy cut short leaves an image that has the right name but won't boot (`init/Mos.img` on 10.69.144.171, 2026-10-20), and recovering from a reload into it needs the console (#80). So before `working/` is emptied, each image in `init/` must be the same size (`ls -l`) as its copy in `working/` or in `certified/`. After the copy, every file in `working/` must be the same size as in `init/`. Otherwise the Cleanup doesn't reload. An image in `init/` that matches neither copy is first sent afresh (#117): the release `init/` says it holds (`show microcode init`) is installed into `init/` and `certified/` as ADR 0011 installs one, whatever `BLAB_AOS_RELEASE_MODE` says, since it is a repair and not a release change. It covers a copy cut short, and a user who put another release in `working/` and `certified/` by hand (8.11.162.R01 over the Golden init's 8.9.221.R03 on .138 and .139, 2026-11-05). Only if that install fails does the Cleanup refuse. Onboarding checks its own copy into `init/` the same way. Sizes catch a truncated copy, and they cost nothing, where an `md5sum` of a 240 MB image on flash takes a while. BLab keeps no reference of its own for init's image, so another release put in `init/` by hand must go in `certified/` as well. A release the Cleanup installs (#48, ADR 0011) goes into `certified/` as well, checked against the image BLab downloaded. A path or user it can't remove is a warning in the Cleanup's history, and the Inspection after the Cleanup finds it again.

A Switch that powers up again (a power cut, a hard reset) boots from `certified/`, not from `working/`. The Cleanup's shell copy of `init/` into `certified/` before the reload isn't enough. A failure there is only logged, and AOS doesn't count it as certified: `show running-directory` still says `CERTIFY NEEDED` afterwards. Three lab Switches came back from a power cut without the Golden init's static routes (#99). So once the Inspection after a Cleanup finds the Switch clean, the Switch worker runs `write memory flash-synchro`. It saves the running config into `working/` and copies `working/` into `certified/` on every chassis of a VC. BLab then waits until `show running-directory` says `CERTIFIED`. On 10.69.144.132 (AOS 8.11.162.R01, 2026-11-03) the command asked nothing, printed nothing and took 43 s, and the status changed a little after it returned. Right after a reload AOS may refuse it for a while (`not permitted during initialization and CMM synchronization`, or `has not reached the ready state`), even once `user` commands answer, so BLab tries again for about 5 minutes, waiting 10 s and then twice as long each time, up to 60 s (#106). Onboarding does the same after its first clean Inspection. BLab doesn't run it on a Switch that runs from `certified/`, because then `working/` didn't boot, nor on one that isn't clean. If it fails, that is a warning on the Cleanup, never a reason, and the next Cleanup tries again. Its outcome goes into the Cleanup's entry of the history.

When all an Inspection after a Cleanup finds is what that Cleanup should have repaired (Unwanted files, local users, a changed init or VC setup, auto-VFL ports, Switch accounts), BLab failed rather than the holder. So the Quarantine names nobody, and an admin clears it. The Sweep counts the same findings as a change since the last Cleanup: with `BLAB_SWEEP_MODE=enforce` it Cleans the Switch up, and in either mode they never Quarantine it (#101).

## Rollout: `BLAB_CLEAN_STATE_MODE`

Pre-prod drives the real Switches (ADR 0002), so this would reach the lab as soon as it is on `dev`. The lab must be told first, and the extras must list the lab managers' logins first (monitoring users such as `observium` or `snmp-user` exist on several Switches). So the mode starts at `report`, the default. In `report`, a Cleanup only notes in the Switch's history what it would remove or rewrite (`Would remove (BLAB_CLEAN_STATE_MODE=report): ...`), and an Inspection lists the findings as warnings. In `enforce`, the Cleanup repairs and the Inspection fails on what is left. Titouan fills the extras, announces it, and then sets `enforce` in both `.env` files.

## Considered options

- **Protect `init/` and `/flash` on the Switch.** AOS has no permission per folder, and read-only Switch accounts would stop users configuring the Switch they reserved.
- **Remove everything not in a short list, with no extras.** That would remove the monitoring logins and the lab managers' tools on every Release.
- **Remove the auto-VFL ports at run time after each Cleanup** (`no virtual-chassis auto-vf-link-port <port>`, which works on 132 at once) and save them. Each Cleanup would empty `working/` and bring them back at the reload, and BLab would repair them again on every Switch every time.
- **Compare `working/` and `certified/` too.** AOS writes files there that vary by family and release (`vcsetup.cfg`, `imgsha256sum`, `appmon_vcboot.cfg`...), which would bring false Quarantines. The Cleanup replaces both from `init/` anyway.
- **Keep other local users as a warning (ADR 0004).** The issue's rule is that nothing of a Reservation stays. A login made by hand keeps a way in after the Reservation ends.

## Consequences

- This amends ADR 0004: in `enforce` mode, local users other than `admin` and the extras fail the Inspection and are removed at every Cleanup.
- Crash dumps (`pmd/pmd-*`), histories and user folders are gone after a Release. Anyone who needs to keep something on every Switch asks an admin to add it to the extras.
- `show interfaces` on AOS 8.10.86 prints the port after the colon (`Chassis/Slot/Port : 1/1/1`). The Inspection now reads both forms. Before this, every Inspection of 132 since its upgrade was `unreadable: show interfaces`.
- Production runs this only once `main` has it. Until then, a Cleanup through production's worker restores init the old way.
- Each AOS release keeps its local users in a table of its own (`/flash/system/userTable<n>`), and a `user` command writes it at once, with no `write memory` (checked on 132, AOS 8.10.86.R04, 2026-10-20). A Cleanup that installs another release, as the first one after the Golden init's release changes does, boots into that release's older table. Users the Cleanup removed come back (`palani` on 155, #74), and so may an older `admin` password (169). So once the Switch is up, the Switch worker removes the other local users again before the Inspection that ends the Cleanup. BLab never changes `admin`, so `BLAB_SWITCH_ADMIN_PASSWORDS` keeps the old passwords for as long as a Switch's other releases may hold them.
